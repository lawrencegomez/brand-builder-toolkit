# "What you just learned" cards

Show the matching card at the end of each phase, word for word or lightly adapted to
their project. Keep it short. The goal is that after one project the person can run
the same process on their own next time.

Format:

> **What you just learned: <skill>**
> <2-3 sentences, plain English, specific to what we just did>
> **Try it yourself next time:** "<a sentence they can type to their agent>"

---

## Phase 1: Discovery
**Skill: briefing an AI like a creative director.** A good website starts with clear
answers to who it is for, what they should feel, and what they should do. Vague briefs
get generic sites. The recommended-answer format lets you steer fast instead of
writing essays.
Try: "Grill me about my brand before you design anything."

## Phase 2: Inspiration 🔎
**Skill: analyzing reference websites for design direction.** You do not copy a site
you admire, you take specific ingredients from it: its type pairing, its spacing, the
way its hero tells the story. Pulling the real fonts, colors, and spacing out of a site
turns "I like this vibe" into instructions an AI can follow.
Try: "Break down why <url> works and what we should borrow from it."

## Phase 3: Platform
**Skill: picking the right home before building.** Custom code gives you the most
control; Squarespace, Shopify, or Framer give you the easiest editing. The right answer
depends on who updates the site and what it needs to sell.
Try: "Given how I run my business, where should my site live?"

## Phase 4: Typography 🔤
**Skill: choosing type with intent.** Fonts carry most of a brand's personality. A
pairing is one font with character for headlines and one easy-to-read font for body
text, used at a few fixed sizes. Seeing real specimens side by side beats picking
from a list of names.
Try: "Show me three font pairings for my brand as real specimens."

## Phase 5: Design system 🎨 📐
**Skill: designing with a system, not a prompt.** Instead of asking for "a nice
page", we defined the building blocks first: colors with jobs, a type scale, spacing
steps, buttons, cards. Every page is built from those blocks, so the site stays
consistent and a single change updates everything.
Try: "Build this new section only from my design system tokens."

## Phase 6: Structure and copy 🤖 ✍️
**Skill: AI-assisted site structure and copy frameworks.** A sitemap and page outlines
come before words, and words come before visuals. Frameworks like PAS or StoryBrand
give copy a shape that persuades. The humanizer pass removes the phrases that make
writing sound machine-made.
Try: "Rewrite my About page using the StoryBrand framework, then humanize it."

## Phase 7: Visual assets 🖼️
**Skill: making unique graphics and video, cheapest tool first.** Your own photos,
code-drawn graphics, and code-rendered video (Remotion) are free and fully yours.
Paid generators like Higgsfield are for the shots nothing else can make, with a
budget agreed up front.
Try: "Can we make this animation in code before we pay for a generator?"

## Phase 8: Mockups and build 🤖
**Skill: comparing real directions before committing.** Seeing two or three
different takes side by side shows you what you like faster than describing it. The
build then follows the chosen direction and the design system exactly.
Try: "Give me three different directions for this page, built from my tokens."

## Phase 9: Design loop 🔁
**Skill: the design loop.** Screenshot, critique, fix, screenshot again. Each round
targets the biggest problem first. Two or three rounds take a page from "fine" to
"good".
Try: "Run the design loop on my home page."

## Phase 10: Launch
**Skill: shipping with a checklist.** Titles, descriptions, share images, alt text,
contrast, speed, and a working contact path. Small things that decide whether people
find the site and trust it.
Try: "Run the launch checklist before we go live."

## Phase 11: Keep improving 📐 🔁
**Skill: growing a site without losing the brand.** New pages start from the design
system, look changes start in Paper (or `DESIGN.md`), and every decision gets logged.
That is how a site stays coherent after fifty edits.
Try: "Add a pricing page using my design system." / "Change the look of my buttons
everywhere."
